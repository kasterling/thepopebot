## Arizona Residency Requirements

### Definition of Arizona Residency

You are considered an Arizona resident if **any** of the following applies:
- You remain in Arizona for **7 months or more** during any calendar year
- You work in Arizona (except seasonal agricultural work)
- You are registered to vote in Arizona
- You place children in school without paying non-resident tuition
- You have a business with an office in Arizona that bases/operates vehicles
- You obtain a state license or pay school tuition fees as an Arizona resident

### Steps to Establish Residency

#### 1. **Establish Physical Domicile**
- Rent or purchase a residence in Prescott Valley
- Sign lease agreement or mortgage documents
- Set up utilities in your name (electric, water, gas, internet)
- Use this as your permanent address on all documents

#### 2. **Obtain Arizona Driver License**

**Required Documents:**
- Current out-of-state driver license
- Proof of authorized presence in U.S. (birth certificate, U.S. passport, or passport card)
- Social Security number (card not required but number needed)
- **Two documents showing Arizona residency** (utility bills, bank statements, lease agreement, insurance policy)

**Process:**
- Visit any Arizona MVD office (no appointment needed)
- No written or road test required if you have a valid out-of-state license
- Vision exam may be required
- **Travel ID (REAL ID) Option:** Bring two documents with current Arizona address if you want TSA-compliant ID

**Important:** If your legal name differs from documents, bring proof of name change (marriage certificate, divorce decree, court order)

#### 3. **Register Vehicle in Arizona**
If bringing a vehicle from another state:
- Current vehicle title from previous state
- Current vehicle registration from previous state
- **Note:** Phoenix Metro and Tucson may require emissions testing before registration

#### 4. **Update Address Everywhere**
- USPS mail forwarding (moversguide.usps.com)
- Banks and credit cards
- Insurance policies
- IRS and tax authorities
- Voter registration (if desired)
- Social Security Administration

### Timeline for Residency
- **Day 1:** Move to Arizona and establish physical residence
- **Week 1:** Set up utilities, update USPS forwarding
- **Week 2:** Obtain Arizona driver license (within 10 days of establishing residency)
- **Within 30 days:** Register vehicle (if applicable)
- **After 7 months:** Considered permanent resident for all purposes

## Roehl Transport CDL Training Program

### Program Overview

Roehl's "Get Your CDL" program is a **paid, on-the-job training program** where you are hired as an employee from day one. Unlike traditional CDL schools, you don't pay tuition and you earn income throughout the entire training process.

**Training Location:**
```
Roehl Transport - Phoenix Terminal
4909 W Lower Buckeye Rd
Phoenix, AZ 85043
Phone: 715-591-7050
```

### Three-Phase Training Structure

#### **Phase 1: Classroom & Yard Training (3 Weeks)**

**Location:** Roehl Phoenix Terminal  
**Training Ratio:** 3 trainees per 1 instructor  
**Housing:** Provided by Roehl  
**Meals:** Most meals provided

**Curriculum Includes:**
- Basic truck control and vehicle operations
- Vehicle pre-trip inspections
- Coupling and uncoupling trailers
- Yard operations and maneuvering
- Backing and docking procedures
- Speed and space management
- Turning techniques
- Hazard awareness and prevention
- Protective driving techniques
- Communication systems (Qualcomm, ELD)
- Atlas use and trip planning
- Accident procedures
- Life Outside The Truck™ training

**Commercial Learner's Permit (CLP):**
- Support provided for earning your CLP if you don't already have it
- Must take and pass the CLP knowledge test during this phase
- CLP must be held for **at least 14 days** before taking CDL skills test
- CDL skills test taken in the **last week** of Phase 1

**Pay:** You are paid as an employee throughout Phase 1, with payment issued at the end of the third week

#### **Phase 2: Over-the-Road Training (Minimum 19 Days)**

**Format:** One-on-one mentorship with certified Roehl Driver Trainer  
**Important:** This is **NOT** team driving - trainer coaches from passenger seat

**Training Details:**
- Work with live freight on actual routes
- You drive while trainer observes and coaches from passenger seat
- When trainer drives, you observe from passenger seat
- Sleep in truck at truck stops (trucks have separate bunks for trainee and trainer)
- Hands-on experience in real-world conditions
- Skills confirmation at end of Phase 2

**Pay:** Daily rate paid throughout Phase 2, with payment issued 2 weeks after your first day with trainer

#### **Phase 3: Solo Truck & Career Launch (1+ Month)**

**Milestone:** Receive your own truck assignment  
**Support:** Daily contact with dedicated fleet training manager

**Pay Structure:**
- Paid by mileage rate based on selected fleet
- Payment issued one week after completing work
- Continued guidance and support as you develop your skills

**After Phase 3:** Transition to working with a fleet manager in your chosen fleet

### Available Fleet Options in Phoenix Area

**Flatbed National Fleet:**
- **Schedule:** 3 days home after 11-14 days out
- **Routes:** National coverage
- **Equipment:** Flatbed trailers
- Well-suited for Phoenix area drivers

### Income Expectations

**During Training:**
- Phase 1: Employee wages (paid at end of week 3)
- Phase 2: Daily training rate (paid 2 weeks after start)
- Phase 3: Mileage rate (paid weekly, one week after work completed)

**Post-Training (Experienced Drivers - 1+ Year):**
- **Average Weekly:** $1,345-$1,360 ($69,940-$70,720 annually)
- **Top 50% Average:** $1,525-$1,540 weekly ($79,300-$80,080 annually)
- **Top Performers:** Up to $2,000+ weekly ($104,000+ annually)

### Program Requirements

**Basic Qualifications:**
- Must be at least 21 years old (for interstate CDL)
- Valid driver license with at least 1 year of driving experience
- Pass DOT medical examination
- Clean driving record (no major violations)
- Authorized presence in United States
- Pass background check and drug screening

**Medical Certification:**
- DOT medical exam required before starting
- Can be performed by licensed doctor of medicine, doctor of osteopathy, physician assistant, advanced practice nurse, or doctor of chiropractic
- Medical Examiner Certificate must be completed and current

### Benefits Package (Full-Time Employees)

Starting from Day 1:
- Health insurance
- Dental insurance
- Vision insurance
- Life insurance
- Short & long-term disability
- Flexible Spending Account (FSA)
- Employee Assistance Program (EAP)
- 401(k) with profit-sharing
- Paid vacation
- Paid holidays

### Application Process

1. **Fill out application** at www.roehl.jobs or call 715-591-7050
2. **Complete learning process** - Roehl ensures you understand the program and job characteristics
3. **Submit thoughtful, informed application**
4. **Complete screening** - Background check, driving record review, drug test

- [ ] **Schedule DOT Medical Exam** (can be done in Arizona after moving)
  - Find FMCSA-certified medical examiner
  - Cost: $75-$150 (not covered by insurance)
  - Valid for up to 2 years
  - Bring list of medications and medical history

- [ ] **Prepare for Medical Exam**
  - Bring photo ID and Social Security card
  - Bring eyeglasses/contacts if needed (vision test required)
  - Bring hearing aids if applicable
  - List of current medications
  - Medical history documentation (if applicable)
  - Get adequate sleep before exam
  - Avoid caffeine day of exam (blood pressure test)

- [ ] **Medical Exam Requirements**
  - Vision: 20/40 or better in each eye (with correction OK)
  - Hearing: Able to hear forced whisper at 5 feet
  - Blood pressure: Under 140/90 preferred (higher may require short cert)

## Contingency Plans

### Scenario 1: Training Start Date Delays

**If Roehl training is delayed after you've moved:**

**Immediate Actions:**
- Contact Roehl recruiter for revised timeline
- Confirm commitment and maintain communication
- Update apartment complex if move-in date changes

**Financial Mitigation:**
- Seek temporary/part-time work in Prescott Valley
  - Retail: Walmart, Target, local stores
  - Food service: restaurants, coffee shops
  - Warehouse: seasonal positions
  - Gig work: DoorDash, Uber Eats (if available)
- Reduce discretionary spending
- Use emergency fund if needed
- Negotiate payment plan with landlord if necessary

**Productive Use of Time:**
- Continue CDL manual study
- Take additional practice tests
- Improve physical fitness
- Explore Prescott Valley area
- Network with local truckers
- Research additional endorsements to pursue

### Scenario 2: Medical Exam Issues

**If you don't pass DOT medical exam initially:**

**Common Issues & Solutions:**

**High Blood Pressure:**
- See primary care doctor for management
- May receive 3-month medical card (re-test after treatment)
- Reduce sodium, exercise, manage stress
- Medication may be prescribed

**Vision Issues:**
- Get corrective lenses
- Re-test with glasses/contacts
- LASIK surgery option (longer-term)

**Hearing Issues:**
- Get hearing aids
- Re-test with aids
- Some exceptions available

**Diabetes:**
- Must be well-controlled
- May require endocrinologist letter
- Insulin users need additional certification
- Non-insulin dependent easier to certify

**Sleep Apnea:**
- CPAP machine may be required
- Compliance monitoring needed
- Provide CPAP usage reports
- May receive 1-year card (vs. 2-year)

**Action Plan:**
- Get issues addressed immediately
- Provide medical documentation
- Re-test as soon as cleared
- Communicate with Roehl about delay

### Scenario 3: Training Program Challenges

**If you struggle during CDL training:**

**Academic Challenges (Knowledge Tests):**
- Request additional study time
- Work with instructor one-on-one
- Use online practice tests extensively
- Form study group with other trainees
- Focus on weak areas
- Don't rush - accuracy over speed

**Skills Challenges (Driving/Backing):**
- Request extra practice time
- Watch experienced drivers
- Break down maneuvers into smaller steps
- Practice mental visualization
- Stay calm and focused
- Remember: everyone learns at different pace

**Personal Challenges:**
- Homesickness: Schedule calls home, focus on goal
- Physical discomfort: Adjust seat/mirrors, take breaks
- Sleep issues: Maintain schedule, use earplugs/sleep mask
- Stress: Talk to trainers, practice deep breathing

**Communication:**
- Be honest with instructors about struggles
- Ask questions - no question is stupid
- Request clarification if confused
- Advocate for yourself
- Stay positive and persistent

**Remember:** Roehl invested in you - they want you to succeed!

### Scenario 4: Failing CDL Test

**If you fail CDL skills test:**

**Immediate Response:**
- Don't panic - many people retake
- Review what went wrong with instructor
- Identify specific areas needing work
- Schedule re-test (must wait 1 business day)

**Improvement Plan:**
- Focus practice on failed elements
- Request additional yard time
- Mental preparation and visualization
- Manage test anxiety
- Get adequate rest before re-test

**Roehl Policy:**
- Typically allows multiple test attempts
- May extend training period
- Continued support from instructors
- Discuss options with training coordinator

**Worst Case:**
- If can't pass after multiple attempts, may need to separate
- Not the end of trucking career
- Can try with different training program
- Consider additional private training
- Address root cause (anxiety, skills, comprehension)

### Scenario 5: Financial Emergency During Training

**If unexpected expenses arise during training period:**

**Immediate Resources:**
- Use emergency fund
- Contact family for short-term loan
- Negotiate payment plans with creditors
- Reduce all non-essential spending

**Income Options:**
- Training pay continues - ensure you receive all payments
- Sell unnecessary possessions
- Request advance on training pay (ask Roehl)
- Short-term personal loan (last resort)

**Budget Cuts:**
- Cancel subscriptions (streaming, gym, etc.)
- Meal prep vs. eating out
- Reduce utility usage
- Skip entertainment expenses
- Defer non-urgent purchases

**Communication:**
- Talk to Roehl if severe hardship
- May have hardship assistance options
- Maintain transparency
- Focus on completing training to get to better-paying Phase 3

### Scenario 6: Vehicle Breakdown

**If personal vehicle breaks down:**

**Immediate Actions:**
- Assess if repair is economical
- Get quotes from multiple mechanics
- Determine if vehicle is needed right now

**Considerations:**
- Phase 1: Need vehicle for commuting or staying in Phoenix lodging
- Phase 2-3: Will be in truck, personal vehicle not needed
- Can defer major repairs until earning more money
- May not need personal vehicle immediately

**Alternatives:**
- Carpool with other trainees during Phase 1
- Use rideshare (Uber/Lyft) temporarily
- Stay at Phoenix lodging vs. commuting
- Buy inexpensive used car ($2,000-$3,000)
- Finance cheap reliable vehicle
- Wait until Phase 3 income to replace

**Long-Term:**
- Once earning steady income, can afford better vehicle
- May not need vehicle much due to trucking schedule
- Consider if vehicle is really necessary

### Scenario 7: Housing Issues

**If problems with Prescott Valley apartment:**

**Common Issues:**
- Maintenance problems: Document and report immediately
- Noise complaints: Talk to neighbors, then management
- Safety concerns: Report to management and police if needed
- Rent increase: Negotiate, or prepare to relocate
- Bad landlord: Know tenant rights, document everything

**Arizona Tenant Rights:**
- Landlord must maintain habitable conditions
- Must provide notice before entry (usually 48 hours)
- Can't retaliate for complaints
- Security deposit return rules (14 days)
- Resources: azlawhelp.org

**Relocation Options:**
- Other apartments in Prescott Valley
- Nearby Prescott (slightly more expensive)
- Chino Valley (more affordable option)
- Dewey-Humboldt (smaller, cheaper)
- Room-for-rent situations (temporary)

**Cost Considerations:**
- Breaking lease may cost 1-2 months rent
- Moving costs for local move
- New security deposit
- Weigh costs vs. staying in bad situation

### Scenario 8: Career Dissatisfaction

**If you decide trucking isn't for you:**

**During Training:**
- Talk to trainers about concerns
- Evaluate if temporary adjustment or fundamental mismatch
- Consider if specific issues (company, schedule) vs. entire career
- Remember initial negative cash flow already invested

**After Training:**
- Complete commitment to Roehl (typically 12 months)
- Leaving early may require repayment of training costs
- Review contract carefully
- Consider switching fleets within Roehl (local, regional)

**Alternative Paths:**
- Local driving (home daily) - different lifestyle
- Regional driving (home weekends) - better work-life balance
- Different trucking segment (tanker, flatbed, specialized)
- Different company after commitment period
- Leverage CDL for other opportunities (bus driver, local delivery)

**Exit Strategy:**
- Complete contract period if possible
- Leave on good terms (recommendation valuable)
- Save money during trucking to fund next step
- Have new job/plan before quitting
- Consider trucking as stepping stone to other goals

### Scenario 9: Family Emergency

**If family emergency requires you to leave Arizona:**

**During Training:**
- Notify Roehl immediately
- Discuss options: leave of absence, postponement, etc.
- May be able to resume training later
- Family emergency policy may apply

**After Training:**
- Trucking offers flexibility to be near family
- Can request routes/loads going to home area
- May be able to transfer to different Roehl terminal
- Take time off if needed (unpaid after vacation used)

**Long-Term:**
- Some family issues resolve quickly
- Others may require career change
- Evaluate if family can relocate to Arizona
- Consider local driving options near family

**Plan:**
- Maintain emergency contacts updated with Roehl
- Have family communication plan
- Keep emergency fund for sudden travel
- Don't make permanent decisions based on temporary emotions

### Scenario 10: Better Opportunity Arises

**If you receive better job offer during training/first year:**

**Considerations:**
- Roehl training investment - may owe repayment
- Contract obligations (typically 12 months)
- Short-term thinking vs. long-term career
- Grass isn't always greener

**Evaluate Opportunity:**
- Compare total compensation (pay, benefits, time off)
- Research new company thoroughly
- Consider work-life balance implications
- Get offer in writing
- Calculate financial impact of breaking contract

**Decision Framework:**
- If offer is significantly better AND you can afford contract buyout: Consider it
- If offer is marginally better: Probably not worth it
- If offer is in different industry: Weigh career change carefully
- If offer is from competitor: Read Roehl contract for non-compete

**Recommended Approach:**
- Complete initial commitment to Roehl (build experience)
- Re-evaluate at 12-month mark with experience
- Leverage experience for better opportunities later
- Avoid burning bridges - trucking industry is small
