## Executive Summary

The corn market is experiencing mixed signals with **prices consolidating in the $4.30-$4.42 range** for near-term futures contracts. While **record U.S. export demand** is providing underlying support (USDA raised exports by 100 million bushels to a record 3.3 billion), the market faces headwinds from **large global supplies** and ongoing **South American production pressure**. Ethanol demand shows recent recovery but remains below projections, while tariff uncertainties with China continue to add volatility. Technical indicators suggest a cautiously bullish near-term outlook with resistance at $4.40-$4.45 representing a critical breakout level.

## 3. Weather Impact Analysis

### South America (Critical for Global Supply)

#### Brazil - Mixed Conditions
**First Crop (Soybeans being harvested):**
- Heavy rains in central Brazil (Mato Grosso, Goias, Mato Grosso Do Sul) over past week
- Soybean harvest: 33.9% marketed (slower than prior year), ~16% physically harvested
- Concerns about excessive wet weather impacting harvest quality

**Safrinha Corn (Second Crop - Key for Market):**
- **Planting Progress:** 2.8% ahead of last year (positive)
- **Emergence:** 45.8% of planted crop has emerged; 54.2% in vegetative stage
- **Near-term Forecast (Feb 15-21):**
  - Rain showers decreasing this weekend after widespread coverage
  - Lighter, isolated showers early next week allowing field work to resume
  - **Critical Pattern Change Next Week:** Front moves north Wednesday bringing widespread scattered showers
  - Forecast: 30-60mm (1.2-2.4 inches) rainfall, 10mm above average
- **Late February Outlook:**
  - Precipitation likely to remain above average in central Brazil
  - 50-75mm (2-3 inches) expected last week of February
  - Southern Brazil may dry out as high pressure develops
- **Risk Assessment:**
  - January saw significant rainfall deficits (>75mm in key areas)
  - Early planted corn went into dry soil
  - Late February rain beneficial for moisture but may delay planting
  - **Critical Planting Window:** If safrinha planted too late, crop vulnerable when wet season ends (March/April)

#### Argentina - Improving but Damage Done
- Several rounds of rain forecast this week (Feb 10-15)
- Focus on central and northern areas; south remains drier
- Active weather pattern expected to continue next week
- **Assessment:** Improvements too late for advanced corn and soybean crops; six weeks of deteriorating conditions caused permanent damage
- Soil moisture and crop conditions have worsened significantly over past month and a half

### U.S. Weather
- **Current:** Above-normal temperatures spreading across Midwest through next week
- **Soil Moisture:** Not particularly promising; many dry and drought spots exist
- **Winter Wheat Concerns:** Ongoing drought in Plains could impact development
- **Active Pattern Developing:** Multiple systems forecast, potential for bigger storms next week
- **Risk:** Limited precipitation may threaten wheat going into spring; spotty coverage expected

**Current Status:**
- Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins calling on Congress to pass year-round E15 legislation
- Trump administration "has gone as far as we can regulatorily"
- Described as solution for extending supplies and reducing pump prices

**Potential Impact:**
- Could create new demand for **>2 billion bushels** of corn and sorghum
- Estimated consumer savings: **10-30 cents per gallon** at pump
- National Corn Growers Association (NCGA) expressing "alarm" over large crop and calling for year-round E15
- Currently fuel with 15% ethanol cannot be sold year-round due to seasonal restrictions

## 8. Conclusion

The corn market is at a critical juncture, balancing **exceptionally strong export demand** against **burdensome supply levels**. The recent USDA decision to raise exports to a record 3.3 billion bushels while cutting ending stocks provides a modestly supportive backdrop, but the seven-year high in carryout continues to cap rallies.

**Key price determinants over the next 30-60 days:**
1. **Brazilian safrinha** planting pace and early development (weather critical)
2. **U.S. export sales** maintaining record pace
3. **China's buying behavior** as South American harvest progresses
4. **Ethanol demand recovery** and potential E15 policy breakthrough
5. **Technical breakout** above $4.40 resistance or failure thereof

**Trading bias:** **Cautiously bullish** in the near term, with prices likely to test $4.40-$4.45 resistance. A sustained breakout could target $4.65, but failure to break through would likely see consolidation back to $4.30 or lower. The **risk/reward favors waiting for a clear breakout** given the dual nature of strong demand vs. large supplies.

**Recommendation for corn market participants:**
- Commercial hedgers: Consider protective strategies given volatility warnings
- Producers: Scale into sales on rallies above $4.40; record crop quality supports execution
- Speculators: Await breakout confirmation above $4.40 with tight stops below $4.30
- Long-term investors: Monitor policy developments (E15, trade deals) for structural demand shifts
